\section{What we did}
Sub-project 1 gave us coloured dots on a person in a light shirt. We turn them into textured 3D meshes
(\texttt{.obj}) in eight ways, on three versions of the scan: VGGSfM (139k dots, cleaned with the person's
outline in the photos; main input), the denser COLMAP MVS (487k), and MVS with 404 stray floating dots removed
(MVS-clean). A shared last step adds texture, scores and
previews, and our web viewer (\texttt{run\_viewer.sh}, no GPU) shows them side by side.

\section{How}
\paragraph{Our own Poisson (no library).}
Each dot gets an outward arrow from its 24 nearest neighbours, made consistent
across the body. The arrows $\mathbf V$ define an inside-or-outside function $\chi$ on a $384^3$ grid whose slope
matches them ($\Delta\chi=\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf V$), plus a pull that keeps the surface on the dots. Its boundary becomes
triangles, and parts far from any dot are trimmed: 1.0\,M triangles in 39\,s.

\paragraph{Making the course methods work on a real scan.}
\textbf{IER} gets 12.5k evenly spread dots, the density it was trained on. \textbf{MeshAnything V2} learned from clean
meshes: raw dots give F@1\%\,0.51, our Poisson mesh via its authors' preparation step 0.987.
\textbf{Point2Mesh} was misled by the direction of our noisy arrows; the paper's option to ignore it lifts F@1\% from
0.65 to 0.84. \textbf{DHSP} first fits the dots but crumples the surface
(NC\,0.65), and its next step (flattening the surface into an image) stalled for 15\,h. Rebuilding a clean,
simpler surface first (3k/6k triangles) lets it finish in seconds: smooth (NC\,0.89), with its own colours.

\paragraph{Three more methods.}
NKSR (a learned field built for real scans), Shape As Points (Poisson made trainable) and 2D Gaussian Splatting
(learned from the 125 photos). With no true 3D model, Table~\ref{tab:results} scores each mesh against its own input
dots.

\section{What we learned}
\textbf{(1) Classic code holds up:} our Poisson ties the best learned methods on VGGSfM; NKSR wins on the denser MVS.
\textbf{(2) Close to the dots is not a good surface:} DHSP's first stage scores F@1\%\,0.999 yet is crumpled, and
IER's triangles disagree about which side is out (red in Fig.~\ref{fig:teaser}). \textbf{(3) The input matters
most:} MeshAnything needs mesh-like input; Point2Mesh falls apart on MVS with its stray dots (F@1\%\,0.13) and partly
recovers without them (0.39). \textbf{(4) Closed meshes print:} SAP, DHSP, Point2Mesh and our untrimmed Poisson are watertight, exported as 180\,mm STL files.
